# The L1 shield: why one shield can protect the whole day side of Earth

Background for the Act 3 shield in Project Dawn (the Navigator's "aim the shield at L1" task).

## The idea

Can a satellite block sunlight from Earth so that the night side is not covered, but every place is
covered while it is in daylight, thanks to Earth's rotation?

**Yes. That is exactly what a shield at the Sun–Earth L1 point does.**

## Why it works

| Fact | What it means for the shield |
|---|---|
| Sunlight only reaches Earth's **day side**. | The night side faces away from the Sun, so it gets no sunlight and needs no shield. |
| L1 lies on the straight line between the Sun and Earth, about **1.5 million km** from Earth toward the Sun. | A shield there is always in the path of the sunlight heading for Earth. |
| An object at L1 goes around the Sun once a year, **together with Earth**. | The shield stays between the Sun and Earth all year without chasing anything. |
| Earth **rotates** once a day underneath. | Every place on Earth moves from night into day, and so moves under the shield's path. Each city is covered exactly during its daytime. |
| From L1, the half-shadow (penumbra) of a shield is about the **same size as Earth**. | One shield can cover the whole day side at once. |

```
   SUN  ───────────────►  [ SHIELD at L1 ]  ──────►  ( EARTH )
                           1.5 million km             day side faces the Sun
                           from Earth                 night side faces away
                                                      Earth spins: every place
                                                      passes through the day side
```

## Why not a satellite close to Earth?

A satellite in low Earth orbit circles the planet every hour or two. It would only shade a small spot
for a few moments, then move on. A shield at L1 stays lined up with the Sun and covers the whole day
side all the time.

## Real-world catches

- **It dims, it does not make a dark shadow.** From 1.5 million km away, a shield only weakens sunlight
  a little, evenly over the day side. The best-known study (Roger Angel, 2006) aimed to block
  **about 1.8%** of sunlight.
- **It is huge.** That design used **about 16 trillion** small, thin, transparent "flyers" (each about
  60 cm across and about 1 gram), forming a cloud about **100,000 km long**. The total shading area is
  in the millions of square kilometres.
- **Sunlight pushes on it.** Light pressure moves the balance point, so the real design sits a little
  closer to the Sun (about 1.8 million km from Earth). The flyers bend light aside instead of
  reflecting it, which keeps that push small.
- **L1 is not perfectly stable.** The shield needs small steering corrections to stay in place
  (the design uses tiny tilting mirrors as sails).
- **Real proposals dim all sunlight, not only UV.** They were designed to cool the planet. A shield
  that blocks only UV, as in Project Dawn, uses the same geometry but is a game idea: nobody has
  proposed or tested a UV-only filter at this scale.

## Where real spacecraft already use L1

L1 is a real working spot: NASA/ESA's **SOHO** and NASA's **ACE** watch the Sun from there, so they see
solar storms before they reach Earth.

## How this connects to the game

| In the game | Real basis |
|---|---|
| The Navigator aims the shield at L1, about 1.5 million km from Earth | The Sun–Earth L1 point |
| Hardened satellites survive the solar storm | Spacecraft beyond Earth use radiation-hardened parts |
| One shield protects the whole bunker side of Earth during the day | The L1 penumbra covers Earth's disc |
| The ozone slowly heals while the shield helps | Real L1 shields only reduce sunlight; the healing in the game is the ozone layer's own recovery |
